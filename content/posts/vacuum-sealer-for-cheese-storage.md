## Why Cheese Goes Bad

Cheese spoils from three enemies:

1. **Mold spores** — ubiquitous in the air and in your fridge. Land on moist cheese, start growing.
2. **Oxygen** — oxidizes fats, dries out surfaces, and allows mold and bacteria to grow.
3. **Moisture imbalance** — too much moisture = slimy, sour surface; too little = dry, cracked rind.

P[last](/blog/how-long-does-vacuum-sealed-food-last)ic wrap is actually a bad long-term solution for cheese. It traps moisture against the surface, which encourages mold and makes cheese taste like plastic. Cheese paper (wax-coated paper) works, but only for a week or two. Vacuum sealing removes oxygen, the primary driver of mold, while keeping moisture consistent.

## How Long Does Sealed Cheese Last?

| Cheese type | Fridge (original wrap) | Fridge (vacuum-sealed) | Freezer (vacuum-sealed) |
|------------|----------------------|----------------------|------------------------|
| Hard cheeses (Parmesan, aged cheddar, pecorino) | 3–6 weeks | 4–8 months | 12+ months |
| Semi-hard (cheddar, gouda, manchego) | 2–4 weeks | 2–4 months | 6–8 months |
| Semi-soft (brie, camembert, havarti) | 1–2 weeks | 2–3 weeks | Not recommended (texture suffers) |
| Blue cheese (stilton, roquefort) | 2–3 weeks | 3–6 weeks | Not recommended |
| Fresh mozzarella, feta | 5–7 days (in brine) | 2–3 weeks (without brine) | Not ideal |

Soft and blue cheeses are tricky for a reason — their flavor comes from active mold cultures and bacteria that need some oxygen to keep developing. Seal them too tight, too long, and they can go bitter or ammoniated.

## How to Vacuum Seal Cheese

The technique varies by cheese type.

### Hard cheeses (cheddar, Parmesan, gouda, manchego)
These are the easiest.

1. Cut into usable portions (what you will eat in 1–2 weeks once opened).
2. Pat dry with a paper towel if the surface is moist.
3. Place in a valve bag. Fold the top edge into a cuff before adding cheese to keep the zip track clean.
4. Zip and seal with a handheld sealer like FreshLock (**-60 kPa** suction is strong enough but not so intense that it crushes a firm block).
5. Store in the cheese drawer or warmest part of the fridge (the door).

### Semi-soft washed-rind and bloomy-rind cheeses (brie, camembert, epoisses)
These need to breathe. Full vacuum can damage the rind and trap ammonia. Two approaches:

- **Partial seal** — stop suction early, before the bag pulls tight against the rind. You are displacing most of the oxygen, not squishing the cheese.
- **Parchment wrap first** — wrap cheese in a layer of cheese paper or parchment, then vacuum seal it. The parchment acts as a buffer so the plastic is not directly against the rind, and the cheese can still micro-breathe.

Sealed semi-soft cheeses still only last 2–3 weeks. Buy them closer to when you will eat them.

### Blue cheese
Blues are alive. The Penicillium mold that gives them flavor keeps working even in the fridge. Vacuum sealing slows it down dramatically, but does not stop it.

- Wrap in parchment first, then seal partially.
- Plan to eat within a month.
- If you open the bag and smell strong ammonia, the cheese has over-ripened and should be discarded (trim any mold, but ammonia is a sign the cheese has broken down).

### Fresh cheeses (mozzarella, feta, goat cheese)
These are high-moisture and best eaten fresh. If you seal them:

- Pat very dry first (excess moisture causes sogginess).
- Feta can be sealed in small chunks without brine for short storage, but texture changes.
- Mozzarella loses its fresh creamy texture in a full vacuum. Eat fresh or use for melting (pizza, pasta) after freezing.

## The "Open and Reseal" Habit

The big advantage of a handheld sealer for cheese is that every time you open a block to cut a slice, you can **reseal it in 15 seconds**. Countertop sealers require making a new bag; handhelds reuse the same valve bag until the zip track wears out.

Build the habit: open the fridge, take out the bag, unzip, cut what you want, zip, reseal, put back. 15 seconds. The cheese stays at peak freshness for weeks.

## Freezing Cheese

Hard cheeses freeze fine for cooking. Thaw in the fridge for 24 hours and use within a few days of opening. Expect some crumbliness after thawing — frozen cheese is best for melting, not for a cheese board.

Soft cheeses do not freeze well — the water content separates and the texture gets grainy.

## What About Mold That Grows Anyway?

If you open a sealed bag and find a patch of surface mold on a hard cheese:

- Cut off at least 1 inch around and below the mold spot.
- The rest is fine (hard cheese is too dense for mold to penetrate deeply).
- Use a fresh bag to reseal the remaining cheese.

On soft cheeses, any mold means the whole piece is done — mold roots spread easily through soft/moist cheese.
