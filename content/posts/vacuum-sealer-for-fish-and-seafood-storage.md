## Why Fish Gets Ruined in the Freezer

Freezer burn happens when frozen food is exposed to dry, oxygen-rich freezer air. Moisture sublimates (evaporates directly from ice) off the surface, leaving behind a dry, discolored, oxidized layer. On red meat this is annoying. On fish it is catastrophic — the delicate oils oxidize fast, producing that strong "fishy" smell and off flavor.

**Vacuum sealing removes nearly all the air** from the bag before [freezing](/blog/vacuum-sealer-for-freezing-vegetables). No oxygen = no oxidation = no freezer burn. Properly sealed, fatty fish like salmon stays fresh in the freezer for 6–9 months (instead of 2–3 months in a regular bag). Lean white fish like cod or halibut can go 8–12 months.

## Step-by-Step: Sealing Fish for the Freezer

1. **Start with fresh fish.** Vacuum sealing does not make old fish fresh. Process fish the same day you buy or catch it.
2. **Pat fillets dry.** Surface moisture creates ice crystals inside the bag. Pat with paper towels until dry to the touch.
3. **Pre-freeze individual fillets (optional but useful).** Lay fillets on a parchment-lined baking sheet and freeze for 1–2 hours until firm. Then bag and seal. This prevents fillets from sticking together and preserves shape.
4. **Portion smart.** One or two fillets per bag — whatever you will eat in a single meal. Repeatedly opening and resealing the same bag defeats the purpose.
5. **Add seasoning if you want.** A sprig of dill, a slice of lemon, a pat of herb butter — add them before sealing for instant "sous vide ready" portions. Avoid over-salting before freezing, as salt can draw out moisture.
6. **Seal.** Place fish in the valve bag, zip, and seal with the handheld. Stop suction when the bag is tight and the fillet is held firmly — do not keep going until it is squashed.
7. **Freeze flat.** Lay bags flat in the freezer until solid, then stack. Flat stacks save space and thaw faster.

## Storing Shellfish

- **Shrimp and prawns:** Peel or leave shells on (shell-on has more flavor), pat dry, seal in meal portions. Frozen: 6–9 months sealed.
- **Scallops:** Ultra-delicate. Pat dry well, pre-freeze on a tray for 30 minutes first, then seal gently. Do not over-suction.
- **Lobster tails and crab:** Cooked or raw, both seal well. Crack claws slightly if needed so air can escape.
- **Mussels and clams (live):** **Do not vacuum seal live shellfish.** They need to breathe. Store live mussels/clams in the fridge in an open container covered with a damp cloth. Vacuum seal only shucked meat or cooked meat.
- **Smoked salmon and lox:** Seals beautifully. Lasts 2–3 weeks in the fridge sealed, vs. 3–5 days in original packaging after opening.

## Thawing Sealed Fish

The best way to thaw sealed fish is to **drop the frozen bag in a bowl of cold tap water** for 20–30 minutes. It thaws fast and evenly. For thicker fillets, move the bag to the fridge overnight.

You can also cook sealed fish directly sous vide from frozen — just add a few minutes to the cook time.

## Cleaning the Sealer After Fish

Fish smell is tenacious. After sealing a batch of seafood:

1. Remove the **detachable drip cup** and wash it in warm soapy water. Do not let fish juice sit in it.
2. Wipe the nozzle with a damp cloth or food-safe sanitizing wipe.
3. Let all parts air-dry completely before reassembling.

---

## What Not to Do

- **Don't seal raw fish and then refrigerate it for days thinking it will stay fresh.** Vacuum sealing helps, but raw fish still needs to be cooked within 1–2 days in the fridge. Freeze it for longer storage.
- **Don't seal fish that smells "off."** Sealing locks odor in but does not reverse spoilage.
- **Don't overfill bags.** Fish juice in the valve ruins the seal and gets into the pump.
- **Don't forget to label.** Write the species, date, and portion size on the bag. You will not remember in August what the unidentified white block is from March.
