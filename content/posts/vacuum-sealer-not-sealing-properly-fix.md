Nothing is more frustrating than a vacuum sealer that will not hold a seal. You portion a week of meals, seal everything, stack it in the freezer, and come back a few days later to find bags loose and freezer burn setting in. Or the pump runs but never seems to pull the bag tight. The good news: **most vacuum sealer problems are not broken parts** — they are small, fixable issues with a simple root ca[use](/blog/how-to-use-handheld-vacuum-sealer).

Here is how to diagnose and fix a vacuum sealer that is not sealing properly, with specific notes for handheld valve-bag sealers like FreshLock.

---

## First: Diagnose the Problem

There are two distinct failure modes, and they have different fixes:

1. **The pump runs but the bag never gets tight** — suction problem (air is leaking somewhere, or pump output is blocked).
2. **The bag seals tight but loses vacuum within minutes/hours** — seal/valve problem (the closure is not holding).

Let's walk through both.

---

## Problem 1: Pump Runs, Bag Won't Tighten

### Cause A: The nozzle isn't making full contact with the valve
Handheld sealers work by pressing the nozzle firmly over the bag's one-way air valve. If the nozzle is tilted, not centered, or not pressed down hard enough, air leaks in around the edges and the pump can never pull a vacuum.

**Fix:** Make sure the nozzle is centered directly over the valve and pressed flush. Hold it straight — not at an angle. You should feel it seat.

### Cause B: The valve or zipper is dirty
Food crumbs, liquid residue, grease, or dust on the valve membrane or on the zip track prevent a tight closure. This is the single most common cause of bad seals.

**Fix:** Wipe the valve and zipper with a [clean](/blog/how-to-clean-handheld-vacuum-sealer), dry or slightly damp paper towel. Make sure both are completely dry before sealing. Pay special attention to the zip track — even a tiny grain of rice holds the track open.

### Cause C: The drip cup is full or not seated
Handheld sealers with a liquid trap (like [FreshLock](/blog/freshlock-vacuum-sealer-review)'s **detachable transparent drip cup**) will lose suction if the drip cup is full, blocked with food particles, or not clicked firmly back into place after cleaning.

**Fix:** Pull off the drip cup, empty it, rinse with warm water, and make sure the air channel is clear. Re-seat it firmly before sealing. The drip cup uses a **mechanical drip-drain** design — if it is not properly in place, the air path is broken and suction drops dramatically.

### Cause D: Bag is damaged or has a pinhole
Bags develop tiny holes from sharp bone edges, punctures from fingernails, or creasing from repeated use.

**Fix:** Submerge the sealed (puffed) bag in a bowl of water and squeeze gently. Bubbles show you the leak. Transfer food to a new bag.

### Cause E: Battery is low
Cordless sealers lose suction power as the battery runs down. If the battery indicator shows low charge, the pump may run but cannot reach full -60 kPa vacuum.

**Fix:** Recharge via USB-C. A full charge takes about 2–3 hours on FreshLock and gives 80–100 seals.

### Cause F: Pump filter is clogged
On FreshLock, there is a small sponge filter behind the drip cup that catches fine particles. Over time it can get saturated with dust or food particles.

**Fix:** Rinse the sponge filter with warm water, let it air-dry completely, and replace. A clogged filter is a surprisingly common cause of weak suction.

## Problem 2: Seals Tight, Then Loses Vacuum

### Cause A: Zipper not fully closed
The zip track is the second line of defense. If even a corner is not clicked shut, the valve holds initially but air gradually seeps in through the zipper.

**Fix:** Run your thumb and forefinger firmly along the entire zip track from one end to the other, pressing hard. You should hear a continuous click. Then run back the other way.

### Cause B: Moisture in the zip or on the valve
Liquids prevent the zip from closing fully and can interfere with the one-way valve membrane. Juicy meats, sauces, and wet produce are the usual culprits.

**Fix:** Before sealing, wipe the zip track and area around the valve with a dry paper towel. For very wet foods, freeze first or use the drip cup. Leave 2–3 inches of headroom below the valve.

### Cause C: Food fermenting or releasing gas
Some foods naturally release gas after sealing — freshly baked bread, raw cruciferous vegetables (broccoli, cabbage, Brussels sprouts), and certain soft cheeses. The bag looks sealed at first, then inflates from inside.

**Fix:** For these foods, either blanch veggies before sealing, wait for baked goods to cool completely, or use a more breathable storage method. Gas release is not a seal failure — it is the food itself.

### Cause D: Old or degraded bags
[Reusable](/blog/reusable-vacuum-sealer-bags-guide) valve bags last many uses, but the zip track wears out and the valve membrane can degrade over time, especially if washed in a dishwasher.

**Fix:** Replace the bag. Bags are cheap; ruined food is not.

### Cause E: Sealing over wrinkles
If the bag film has a wrinkle near the valve or zipper, it creates a tiny channel for air to seep through.

**Fix:** Smooth the bag around the valve and zip area before sealing.

## Regular Maintenance Prevents Most Issues

A clean sealer is a happy sealer. Every 10–20 seals (or after sealing anything wet/seasoned/marinated):

1. Remove the **detachable drip cup** and empty it.
2. Wash the drip cup in warm soapy water.
3. Wipe the nozzle with a damp cloth.
4. Rinse the sponge filter if visible, and let it air-dry.
5. Wipe down the body of the sealer with a damp (not soaking) cloth.
6. Let all parts dry completely before reassembling.

Every few months, check the sealing gasket (the soft rubber ring around the nozzle) for food buildup or wear. A damaged gasket is easy and cheap to replace.

## When It Is Actually Broken

If you have cleaned everything, the battery is full, the bag is new and dry, and you still have no suction — it is possible there is a pump issue or a cracked internal seal. This is rare on quality handhelds, but it happens.
