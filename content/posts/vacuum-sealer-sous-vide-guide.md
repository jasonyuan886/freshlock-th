## Why Sous Vide Requires Vacuum Sealing

Sous vide — French for "under vacuum" — cooks food in a temperature-controlled water bath. The food is sealed in a bag and submerged, where it cooks gently and evenly to the exact target temperature. No overcooked edges, no guesswork.

Here's why the vacuum seal is non-negotiable:

### 1. Heat Transfer Efficiency
Water is an excellent heat conductor, but only if it's in direct contact with the bag. Air pockets inside the bag act as insulation, creating uneven cooking. A proper vacuum seal ensures the bag hugs the food tightly, allowing the water bath's heat to transfer evenly and efficiently.

### 2. No Floating
Air trapped in a bag makes it buoyant. A partially inflated bag will float to the surface of the water bath, exposing the top of your food to air instead of water. This leads to uneven cooking and potential food safety issues. Vacuum sealing removes every last pocket of air, keeping the bag fully submerged.

### 3. Flavor Infusion
When you add herbs, spices, butter, or [marinades](/blog/vacuum-sealing-marinades-guide) to a vacuum-sealed bag, the vacuum pressure forces the seasoning into the food's surface. This is why sous vide steak seasoned with rosemary and garlic tastes more deeply flavored than a steak seasoned the same way and pan-seared.

### 4. Food Safety
Sous vide cooking happens at lower temperatures than traditional methods — often between 130°F and 160°F (54°C–71°C). At these temperatures, the vacuum seal prevents contamination from the water bath and keeps food in a controlled environment where bacterial growth is minimized during the cooking window.

## Sous Vide Temperature and Time Reference

Here's a quick-reference table for common sous vide ingredients. These are guidelines — adjust based on thickness and personal preference.

| Ingredient | Temperature | Minimum Time | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Steak (medium-rare) | 129°F / 54°C | 1 hour | Sear after for crust |
| Steak (medium) | 135°F / 57°C | 1 hour | Sear after for crust |
| Chicken breast | 145°F / 63°C | 1.5 hours | Juicy, tender result |
| Chicken thigh | 165°F / 74°C | 2 hours | Fall-apart tender |
| Pork tenderloin | 140°F / 60°C | 1.5 hours | Sear after |
| Salmon | 125°F / 52°C | 30 min | Delicate — use gentle vacuum |
| Eggs (soft) | 167°F / 75°C | 13 min | No bag needed |
| Eggs (hard) | 170°F / 77°C | 1 hour | No bag needed |
| Carrots | 183°F / 84°C | 1 hour | Add butter and herbs |
| Asparagus | 183°F / 84°C | 15 min | Quick and crisp |
| Short ribs | 165°F / 74°C | 24–48 hours | Transformative — worth the wait |
| Pork belly | 158°F / 70°C | 24–36 hours | Crispy skin after searing |

### Tips for Vacuum Sealing Sous Vide Ingredients

- **For [liquids](/blog/vacuum-sealer-for-sous-vide-liquids-marinades) (marinades, butter)**: Freeze them first into small cubes or use the bag's zip-top to seal partially, then vacuum carefully. The FreshLock's valve system handles small amounts of liquid well, and the **removable drip cup** catches any excess.
- **For delicate fish**: Use the Small valve bag and vacuum in short pulses rather than a single long pull. This prevents the bag from crushing the fillet.
- **For bone-in meats**: Pad sharp bone edges with a small piece of paper towel before sealing to prevent bag punctures during long cooks.
- **For herbs and aromatics**: Add them to the bag before vacuum sealing. The vacuum pressure will infuse their flavors directly into the protein.

## Sous Vide Safety: What You Need to Know

Sous vide is safe when done correctly, but there are important guidelines:

- **Follow temperature minimums**: Cooking below 130°F / 54°C for extended periods can allow bacterial growth. Stick to established time-temperature tables.
- **Don't exceed 4 hours at room temperature**: If you're cooking at lower temperatures (130–135°F), keep total cook time under 4 hours unless you're following a validated long-cook recipe (like short ribs at 24+ hours, which is safe due to pasteurization).
- **Use quality bags**: This is where BPA-free construction matters. The FreshLock valve bags are food-safe and designed for sustained heat exposure within the sous vide temperature range.
- **Sear after cooking**: A quick sear in a hot pan (cast iron works best) adds the Maillard reaction browning that sous vide can't achieve. This also adds a food safety margin by killing surface bacteria.
