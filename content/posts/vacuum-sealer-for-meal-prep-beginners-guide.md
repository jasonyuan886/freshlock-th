## Why Meal Prep Fails (and How Sealing Fixes It)

Most meal prep fails for one boring reason: **food goes bad faster than you can eat it**.

Cooked chicken is only good for 3–4 days in the fridge in a regular container. Leafy greens wilt in 2–3 days. Cooked rice dries out. Cheese molds. You cook on Sunday, eat well Monday and Tuesday, and the rest becomes guilt in a Tupperware.

Vacuum sealing solves this by removing air from the bag before you seal it. With oxygen gone, oxidation slows, mold struggles to grow, and freezer burn stops happening. Food stays fresh **up to 5× longer** in the fridge or freezer, which means a single Sunday session can legitimately carry you through the week (and into the next).

## The Beginner Meal Prep Workflow

You do not need to portion 21 meals on day one. Start with three high-impact moves:

### 1. Pick two proteins
Chicken breasts, ground beef, salmon fillets, or tofu. Season them, seal them individually, and freeze what you will not eat in the next 3 days. FreshLock's **-60 kPa** suction is strong enough to pull a tight, even bag around awkward shapes like chicken thighs without crushing them.

### 2. Prep one carb base
Rice, quinoa, roasted potatoes, or pasta. Portion into single servings (about a cup) and seal flat. Stacking flat-sealed bags in the fridge or freezer saves a ton of space.

### 3. One veg, one sauce
Roasted broccoli, steamed green beans, or a salad mix (keep greens unsealed or only partially sealed — full vacuum crushes leaves). A jar of pesto, tahini, or stir-fry sauce can also be portioned into silicone ice cube trays, frozen, and sealed into serving cubes.

When dinner comes: grab a protein, a carb, a veg. Sous vide, microwave, or pan-sear. Assemble, add sauce, eat. Total time: under 10 minutes.

## Three Beginner Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Sealing warm food.** Let food cool to room temp (or fridge temp) before sealing. Trapping steam creates condensation that causes ice crystals in the freezer and can lead to spoilage.
2. **Overfilling bags.** Leave 2–3 inches of headroom so nothing gets sucked into the valve.
3. **Liquids in the bag.** Sauces, marinades, and juicy meats can get pulled up into the pump. FreshLock's **detachable transparent drip cup** (mechanical drip-drain) catches stray liquid before it hits the motor — just empty and rinse it after sealing wet foods.

## Freezing vs Fridge Storage

A quick rule of thumb:

- **Eat within 3–4 days?** Sealed in the fridge.
- **Longer than that?** Freeze. Seal first, then freeze.
- **Want maximum shelf life?** Freeze in a single layer on a baking sheet first (so portions stay flat and do not stick together), then stack.

Most vacuum-sealed cooked meals last 2–3 weeks in the freezer without losing texture, compared to 3–5 days for loose-wrapped food.

## Building the Weekly Habit

Meal prep fails not because people are bad at cooking, but because they set unrealistic standards. You do not need Instagram-worthy stacked containers of pre-portioned quinoa bowls on Sunday. You need three or four things to be ready ahead of time so that when you walk in tired on Wednesday, dinner takes 10 minutes.

A simple first-week routine:

- **Sunday (30 minutes):** Season and seal 4 chicken breasts and 2 salmon fillets, freeze what you will not eat by Thursday. Portion 3 cups of cooked rice into three bags.
- **Wednesday (10 minutes after dinner):** Chop a bag of broccoli, toss with olive oil and salt, seal into two portions.
- **Friday (5 minutes):** Check the fridge. Anything that is not getting eaten in the next 2 days goes into the freezer.

That is it. No system, no calendar, no color-coded containers. The sealer is the safety net: anything you don't eat gets sealed and frozen instead of going to waste.
